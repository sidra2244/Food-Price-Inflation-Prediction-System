Key Insights & Policy Implications
Data-driven findings from 20+ years of Pakistan food price data
Critical Findings
2023 Crisis: Most severe food inflation in two decades
At 47.6% YoY, the 2023 food inflation surge — driven by PKR devaluation, floods destroying crops, and global supply chain disruption — was the worst on record. The national average jumped from ₨210 in 2022 to ₨310 in a single year. Recovery in 2024 was partial, still registering 19.4% inflation.
Cooking oil is the highest absolute price commodity
At ₨900/litre in 2025 and forecast to reach ₨1,227 by 2030, cooking oil represents the steepest absolute cost burden for households. Its strong tie to palm oil import prices (tracked in Global PKR dataset) makes it the most globally sensitive commodity in Pakistan's food basket.
Balochistan consistently 15–20% above Punjab prices
The provincial price disparity reflects logistical costs, market access, and infrastructure gaps. Balochistan's food prices have been structurally 15–20% above Punjab since 2004. This gap is forecast to widen slightly by 2030, suggesting infrastructure investment is needed to reduce food price inequality.
Inflation tapering forecast: 5% by 2028–2030
The model projects a gradual deceleration of food inflation — from 12% in 2025 to ~5% by 2028 — conditional on PKR stability, no major climate shocks, and continuation of current agricultural policies. This aligns with historical post-crisis normalization observed after 2008 and 2011.
Seasonal Patterns
May–June: Lowest prices annually
The wheat harvest season (April–June) reliably depresses food prices nationally. This seasonal effect is captured via cyclical month encoding (sin/cos features) in the XGBoost model.
Oct–Nov: Price uptick before winter
Post-harvest stockpiling, reduced supply, and higher heating/cooking demand typically drive a seasonal price peak in Q4. The seasonal index shows 3% premium in Oct vs June trough.
Model Reliability Notes
What the model does well: Short-to-medium term (1–18 month) forecasts with high accuracy · Seasonal pattern capture · Commodity-level specificity
Known limitations: Cannot predict black swan events (climate disasters, political crises, currency collapses) · Long-horizon (3–5 year) forecasts carry ±15–20% uncertainty · Model was trained on 2004–2022, so 2023 crisis is partially out-of-distribution
Recommended use: 6–18 month operational forecasts · Annual budget planning · Policy scenario analysis with explicit uncertainty acknowledgment beyond 2027
